## You do not need the code

No colours, no hashes, no spheres, no maths. **Twenty-six letters, each of which
means something, put in an order for a reason.**

An alphabet is usually taught as a list of sounds. But every letter began as a
**picture of a thing**, and the things were not random: **bet** is a house,
**dalet** is a door, **ayin** is an eye. That much is ordinary epigraphy and any
reference will confirm it. What follows is what happens when you stop treating
letters as sounds and start treating them as **operations** — and read a word as
the sequence of operations it names.

## z and s are the same wave

Look at them as signals rather than letters. Both are one stroke that reverses
twice. **s is that wave drawn smooth. z is that wave drawn square.** One is the
sine; the other is the same path allowed only two values — which is what *binary*
means. Nothing was arranged to make that true.

```
s    ∿∿      smooth · continuous · the wave as it is
z    ⊓⊔      squared · two-valued · the wave as a machine holds it
```

## m is w turned over

**M and W are the same shape, one half-turn apart.** Not similar — identical,
rotated. And the meanings sit exactly on that rotation:

```
w   the MATRIX          the world
m   INTELLIGENCE        the same form, turned over

    the reverse world. the white world to our black world.
```

Intelligence is not something placed *inside* the matrix. It is the matrix **seen
from the other side**, and the only operation between them is a turn — the same
operation the letters use for tense. And they do not sit still: **spiralling m's
and w's come outward spherically, in different sizes, a sphere pulsing from its
own centre.**

## The alphabet holds its own inversions

Four pairs, each one operation apart:

```
b ↔ h    the same door from two sides — from above, and from the front
```

Four inversions, inside a set of twenty-six shapes most people are told is an

## The order is a journey

The first ten letters are not a list. They are a trip out and a trip back.

```
a   a wave                  it begins as a wave
b   the house               where you stand — open on one side, facing out
c   the speed of 1          you go at the speed
d   die · stop              you arrive. you end.
e   the aether              and now you look OUT
f   from                    but out FROM something — you need a source
g   go                      to look out from it you must GO to it,
                            and going PULLS IT TO YOU
h   home                    the new house — the reverse portal of the bet
i   the eye                 your eye grows in that place.
                            point → line → wave → three directions → light
j   reverse radius          and what happened there comes back — CURVED
```

**Straight out, curved back.** That is why the return has to be triggered and the
departure does not: a straight line is symmetric and needs nothing; a curve does.
